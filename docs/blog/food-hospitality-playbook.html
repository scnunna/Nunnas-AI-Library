From menu to vertical video: an AI playbook for food and hospitality

Restaurants, caterers and food creators share one problem: they need a steady stream of appetising video and almost no time to make it. Here is how the tools in this library combine to solve it.

cross-industryvideofoodsmall-business

Food is the most visual category on short-form platforms, and it is also run by the busiest people: chefs, franchise owners and caterers who cannot stop service to film. The pain point is volume. One good video a month does nothing; one a day builds an audience.

The five-scene recipe short

The format that works is simple and repeatable:

  1. Hook: the finished dish at its best, with a time promise ("better than takeout in 30 minutes").
  2. Prep: the one step people get wrong, with a real quantity.
  3. Heat: the sear, the sizzle, the moment of transformation.
  4. Build: the sauce or the assembly.
  5. Serve: the money shot and a call to follow.

Each scene is a still keyframe (food photography from an image model), animated for about six seconds by an image-to-video model, then stitched with voiceover, captions and a music bed. A recipe card can be converted into all five prompts by an LLM in seconds.

Beyond recipes

  • Menu launches: one clip per new item, generated from the menu copy and a single phone photo.
  • Catering sales: a per-client highlight reel for proposals, which turns a PDF quote into something people share.
  • Franchise consistency: one template, many locations. Each store gets its own clips with the same brand look.
  • Lead capture: a QR code on the table or the box leads to a page that collects the email address, then a newsletter tool does the follow-up.

What to watch

  • Disclose AI visuals. Platforms increasingly require it, and diners care whether the dish on screen is the dish on the plate. Use real photos of signature items wherever you can.
  • Keep the voice consistent. A single narrator voice across every video builds recognition faster than variety.
  • Measure what matters: saves and shares beat views for food content.

The cross-functional lesson

Notice that the same chain serves marketing (clips), sales (proposal reels) and operations (training videos for new kitchen staff). Teams usually buy three separate tools for this. One pipeline with three templates is cheaper and keeps the brand consistent.
